PCB 2.0 fabrication review — 2026-10-15
Independent review; no canonical design files edited; no fabrication release.

Recommendation
==============
Use the existing 0.45 mm thermal-land candidate as the layout baseline. Keep
four copper layers, 1.6 mm finished thickness, the present copper weights,
ENIG, and the present 24 filled/copper-capped thermal holes. Integrate USB-C
on a lateral extension if practical so the board remains below 100 mm on its
long axis. Keep the core placement, copper returns and power loops intact.
The final outline needs a new quote; the old 99 x 30 mm price is not a price
for the integrated board.

This is the lowest-change, defensible route. It removes the separate 0.6 mm
USB PCB and its expensive fabrication, while retaining the established
soldering assumptions under the two power ICs. Do not claim that US$450 is
unavoidable for safety, or that all QFNs universally require filled vias.
The saved prices already show a much cheaper supplier for the same process.

Can fill/cap be removed?
=======================
Not as an unqualified manufacturing-option change for this revision.

TI BQ25895 section 11.1 requires the exposed power pad to be soldered to
ground and sufficient thermal vias directly underneath to other ground
layers. TPS63020 section 10.3 likewise relies on thermal coupling through
its soldered exposed pad. Neither datasheet makes epoxy fill universally
mandatory. They do support retaining the current pad and heat paths.
https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/bq25895.pdf
https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/tps63020.pdf

TI QFN/SON attachment guidance (SLUA271C, sections 3.4.1 and 3.6) describes
several workable via treatments: plugging, tenting and some open-via OSP
processes. Smaller holes reduce solder-volume loss, but backside tenting
can increase trapped-air voiding. TI calls for process development and
fabricator-specific advice. Thus an ordinary open/tented-via design could
be viable, but equivalence of solder quality is not demonstrated by DRC.
https://www.ti.com/lit/an/slua271b/slua271b.pdf

In this actual board, 12 of 24 thermal bores overlap paste apertures in the
saved exported geometry (eight U201, four U101). The chosen filled/capped
process avoids solder draining through those holes. Resin is not the main
heat conductor: the plated copper barrels and ground copper are. Removing
fill chiefly changes solder-process behavior; it cannot be described as
retaining identical cooling without checking the resulting solder joint.

JLC distinguishes ink plugging from epoxy fill/cap. It does not offer ink
plugging for exposed pad holes or holes within 0.35 mm of pad openings;
it also distinguishes component-pad holes from routed vias. Our thermal
holes are represented as plated pads, so an explicit coordinate list is
needed. A generic "tented vias" dropdown does not override the large open
thermal-pad mask aperture. Require supplier confirmation of the selected
thermal holes and cap flatness for this exact build.
https://jlcpcb.com/help/article/pcb-via-covering

A later no-fill experiment would need coordinated thermal-pad mask/stencil
design, an agreed assembler process, solder-volume/void inspection and
worst-case load/charging temperature measurements. Moving all thermal vias
outside the pads is a larger thermal/layout change and is not recommended
under the present minimal-change instruction.

Three previously selected signal holes are now outside the solder pads
after PR #36. Their saved fill/cap flags are stale requirements, not evidence
that the holes still lie in-pad. Removing only these flags does not remove
the paid process while the 24 thermal holes remain. Leave them unchanged
for this baseline, or explicitly classify them as optional after comparing
final CAM. Never reuse the older pre-PR coordinate list without translating
and verifying it against the current board.

Cost and manufacturing settings
===============================
The saved 12 October calculator results are for FIVE bare main PCBs, no
shipping, tax, components or assembly:
  Original ENIG + four-wire Kelvin test:          USD 82.35
  0.45 candidate ENIG + same Kelvin test:         USD 65.44
  0.45 candidate ENIG + ordinary full e-test:     USD 48.70
  0.45 candidate OSP + ordinary full e-test:      USD 31.74
Source files: cost-reduction/price-comparison.json and uploaded quote captures.
Original separate USB PCB: USD 70.57 for five, before shipping/tax.

The repeatable PCB geometry saving is USD 16.91 from the larger thermal
lands with all other choices held constant. Current JLC capability and
quote pages confirm a 0.20 mm via hole with >=0.45 mm copper diameter
avoids the small-via surcharge on multilayer boards. Specify actual 0.20 mm
drills in order notes; do not let a default category enlarge them to 0.30 mm.
The factory still must accept the thermal holes as vias for this pricing.
https://jlcpcb.com/capabilities/pcb-capabilities/
https://cart.jlcpcb.com/quote

Retain 4 layers and the original layer order/dielectric/copper dimensions.
Use ordinary through-vias; no blind/buried vias, backdrilling, edge plating,
controlled-impedance service or custom stack unless a documented electrical
requirement emerges. New USB extension vias should be at least 0.45/0.20 mm
(copper/drill); prefer larger where placement allows. Preserve original
design clearances, trace widths and existing local rules rather than relaxing
them to make new routing pass. Use ordinary routed edges and standard green
mask. Price the final actual dimensions, finish, full e-test and process.

Four-wire Kelvin is an optional resistance-sensitive fabrication test here.
Standard full opens/shorts testing is a cheaper defensible prototype option,
but it is not identical test coverage. Keep Kelvin if the goal is unchanged
factory inspection. Neither test replaces assembled functional tests.
https://jlcpcb.com/blog/the-guide-to-flying-probe-testing

ENIG is the conservative prototype finish for storage and rework. OSP is a
possible cheaper finish if assembly is prompt and the assembler agrees to
the thermal-cycle and storage limits. JLC lists a typical unopened shelf
life up to three months and four total thermal cycles (two reflow, two wave).
Do not make OSP the default for an indefinite hand-assembly/rework schedule.
https://jlcpcb.com/news/jlcpcb-pcb-osp-finish

Six layers do include fill/cap at JLC, but the saved same-finish calculator
trial cost USD 52.44 versus USD 48.70 for four layers. There is no tested
six-layer layout. Changing layers just to obtain "free vias" is not a proven
saving and conflicts with the minimum-change target.
https://jlcpcb.com/news/free-via-in-pad-6-20-layer-pcbs-pofv

Remaining limits
================
- No final PCB 2.0 fabrication price has been obtained by this review.
- The 400 NOK delivered budget is not established by the old calculator.
- Native DRC/electrical equivalence and actual CAM must pass after integration.
- Supplier acceptance of thermal-hole classification and fill/cap is pending.
- Prototype thermal, charging, transient, ESD and measurement tests remain
  outstanding. The new layout must preserve the project's charging hold.
- PCB supports and USB insertion forces need mechanical validation after the
  enclosure is adapted; a short lateral tab must not become a fragile neck.
